From a work meeting with Gemma on 27 October, while we were planning the Commoners Fair with De Fabriek. Gemma tells how the honest shop worked at the Museum of Arte Útil, and we talk about what it could mean at the fair.

## Transcript

**Gemma:** So, a part, the honest shop, that is a part of those that are going to be present in the space. They could be people that maybe are not able to make it, or whatever, but we would have a spot where people leave their objects or materials that they want to put, and all those that are coming would…

**Minsung:** Speaking of practising trust.

**Gemma:** Yeah, it's about that. It's about that. So you take it, and you put the money, and you just write: I took this, I put this money for it. The people can put the price if they want, or we can make it completely experimental and crazy, that it's really the one that is purchasing who is putting the price. Okay? But initially it's about those who make it putting the price. But nobody is taking care of that shop. Nobody's taking care of that shop. So there is not a shop owner or shop tender. There is nobody there. You come, take whatever you think you like, you put the money in a pot and you just write there: I took this and I put this money.

And normally we did it during the Museum of Arte Útil. That was one of the main rooms, do you remember that? And it was growing so much. We started with 13 and it ended with 70. 70 people putting things there. And I have a very good friend, that her son was making these beautiful cards with drawings, and he put them there. And everybody knew, because you could see the name of the artist, the maker, and read that he was a kid. So a lot of people put more money, paid more than the value that they put on the cards, because they really valued the fact that he was so young, and it was very beautiful.

**Minsung:** That's also what I hope, that we inspire people. That we really pull into their goodwill. That the monetary value they put on it is an expression of…

**Gemma:** …their support. Exactly.

**Minsung:** Yeah. Not the market value.

**Gemma:** Exactly. So that came in with the honest shop, that happened in practice. I mean, it's not part of the concept, but what happened in practice at the museum is that the income was always higher than the real cost of the things, because people were giving more money, because they valued it.

**Minsung:** In our case it is part of our core concept. And I love also that we give another way for people to participate, either out of choice or out of limitation, not being able to be there. And I guess it is another form of practising this alternative economy, a trust-based alternative economy.

**Gemma:** That's what I mean.
