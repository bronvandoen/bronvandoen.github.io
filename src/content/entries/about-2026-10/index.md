We are a Situated Studio, a social design research practice in Eindhoven. We work with people in the places they live, and we use art, design and research to create the conditions for care.

The studio is where our two practices meet. We are Gemma Medina, an art historian, curator and educator who has worked on the Arte Útil archive since the Museum of Arte Útil at the Van Abbemuseum in 2013 and co-organises the Asociación de Arte Útil; and Minsung Wang, a designer in civic service design and urban commoning, whose studio Bron van Doen started Uit de Buurtfabriek, a social furniture workshop, and De Bloemfontein, a neighbourhood hub in Doornakkers. We have worked together since 2015, when we started Agents of Change with the Van Abbemuseum and mapped more than seventy grassroots initiatives across five districts of Eindhoven.

This page expresses what we hold to at this moment.

## Why situated

Knowledge always comes from somewhere. It is made by people in a place, at a human scale, and it finds meaning within that context. So we start where people are: a street, a workshop, a community garden, a school turned into a shared place.

A situation is more than its people. It holds the buildings and the land, the plants and animals, the weather, the money, the rules, and every relation between them. These depend on each other. We think that interdependence is worth celebrating, and worth looking after.

Nowadays, much of life is fragmented. People live side by side without meeting, and the places where they could meet are disappearing. A lot of our work asks how to recover what holds life together: how to give people occasions to gather, to live convivially, and to care for one another and for where they live.

## Care and the commons

Care is the word we keep coming back to. We look for the people already acting out of care, and we trace the threads of care that run between them. Most of it is hidden: under the logic of progress, under the mantra of growth, and under the noise of social media. We explore, pay attention, and connect.

We use our talents as artists, designers and researchers to build structures of care: a space and an occasion to meet, the know-how to start something together, a path to raise modest funding and support. We nudge people to take these and make them their own.

Care is also how we understand the commons. Where people dwell, what counts as common, and who looks after it? Much of what was shared has been enclosed, sold or simply lost.

Commoning, to us, is interdependence in action: people looking after something together, and after each other while they do, exercising their resilience every step of the way. What is often described today as a lack or limitation to abolish, we see as something to rediscover, to cherish and to practise. We shift commoning from an idea to something done together, and build care and interdependence around what people share.

## How we work

We have been working locally in Eindhoven over long stretches of time. We go to where people are, listen before we offer anything, and create the conditions for encounter. Then we let people focus on what they are good at, together.

Art and design are our means. They can question what has become normal, open up a different way of seeing places and people, and encourage them to dream, to act and to celebrate.

Teaching is part of how we work, so we make educational encounters: workshop modules, exercises, interactive tools and co-creations that people keep using after we leave. Recent work includes a module on Arte Útil and urban commoning for researchers and educators at Breda University of Applied Sciences; Economic Cultures, a workshop series on money and fair practice for graduates of Design Academy Eindhoven; and *Nog Hier*, a new chapter of Agents of Change with De Fabriek in Eindhoven.

We work with educators, researchers, cultural institutions, social organisations and local government: people whose work brings them close to communities and places. If that is you, we would like to hear from you.

## This archive

We diligently produce and refine our knowledge through conversation, writing, and intervention, so we publish as we go. This archive holds our research in progress: working notes and audio recordings, often from a conversation between the two of us; the tools and exercises we use in workshops; and prints such as flyers and posters. Not polished, yet presentable.

Entries stay as they were written, with their date. Some are working thoughts and some are settled; each says which. Tags connect entries across projects, and later we want to follow threads by similarity too. When our view changes, we write a new entry and keep the old one. This page is one of them.

The archive is not a portfolio of finished projects, and the tools are not services. We make them as part of our practice of sharing knowledge, so others can learn from them.
