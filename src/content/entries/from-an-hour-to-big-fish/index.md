Some graduates in Economic Cultures were puzzled by the hourly rate. I'm not going to be a freelancer, they said. That doesn't necessarily mean they'll be employed either. Many still want to make substantial cultural projects, the kind that need grants, subsidies or residencies. So by the end of Module 3 I wanted them to see how the first three modules are related to each other. I call it a chain reaction.

It goes like this. Whether or not you'll ever be paid by the hour: if you undervalue an hour of your work, that feeds into your everyday work. You underprice your invoices for your clients, and that becomes a habit. That habit becomes your economic culture. Then, when you write the budget for your most ambitious work, your true BIG FISH, you won't magically start writing a healthy one. You'll refer back to your everyday experience, your own practice of precariousness.

The other chain reaction starts in the same place. If you value your many-sided work fairly in its smallest units, an hour, half a day, a full day, that shows in your invoices. You practise a healthier economic culture. You learn to stand up for yourself, to present your case, to deal with strangers and with situations that are unfair. With that as your habit, putting a price on your ambition becomes something you understand, and can be confident about.

That's why we built the modules as a sequence, and why the tools stay online: to come back to, and to rewind. If a project budget doesn't feel right, look at how you invoice your day-to-day work. If that doesn't sit right either, look at how you value the smallest unit of your work, usually an hour.

And how do you know it's healthy? Without statistics, it's a feel. While you work, is your income in the background, sustaining you, a resource to live your fullest life as a professional and as a private person? Can you celebrate, enjoy, invest in your craft even when nobody pays for it? Then it's healthy. When the numbers come to the foreground and weigh on your work, and worse on your everyday life, pressuring you to compromise on your happiness, you know something is wrong. You don't need an outside measure then. It isn't sustainable, and one day it will catch up with you.
