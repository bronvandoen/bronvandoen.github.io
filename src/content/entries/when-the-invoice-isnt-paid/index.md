On the first evening of Economic Cultures at DAE, the unspoken rules of negotiating came up, and with them the question of where to go when it goes wrong. Here is a hypothetical scenario: Six months ago, a young designer did about 50 hours of work for a Dutch institution. The brief was a conversation and a few emails; nobody signed anything. Then a new director came in, and the institution now says it will not pay.

I have never gone through something like that myself. But I have heard about dealing with a situation like that from colleagues and other designers. Most people who just graduated wouldn't know how, so here they are, written out as a path. None of this is legal advice. Check each step at the source, and ask for help.

First, the work was agreed with the institution, not with the director who left. As far as I understand Dutch law, an agreement doesn't have to be signed to count: the emails, the messages, the brief and the work you delivered show what was agreed. Collect them now, while you still can.

Then the steps, as the government's own guide for entrepreneurs lists them:

1. Call, and ask whether the work was as agreed and the invoice is right. Take notes. If a call doesn't work, write.
2. Send a payment reminder a few days after the payment term. When no term was agreed, it is 30 days, and a government body also has to pay within 30 days. Say that you will charge the statutory interest.
3. Send a formal demand (*aanmaning*): 14 days to pay, the collection costs, and that you will take legal steps. By post and email, registered if you can.
4. Charge the statutory commercial interest and the collection costs. When nothing else was agreed, the costs follow a sliding scale, at least €40.
5. If they ask for time, agree a payment plan, in writing.
6. If they still don't pay, the subdistrict court (*kantonrechter*) handles claims up to €25,000, and you may go without a lawyer. You pay a court fee that depends on the claim; rechtspraak.nl lists the current amounts.

There is time: a business invoice can still be claimed five years after the payment term.

Who can help along the way. The Kunstenbond and the BNO give legal help to their members, so join before you need them. Neither says whether they take on a conflict that started before you joined, so ask before you sign up. The Juridisch Loket is free when your income is low, but for freelancers it only answers questions about your personal situation, and about *schijnzelfstandigheid*, when you are self-employed on paper but really work as an employee. An unpaid business invoice is not one of them.

None of this happens by itself. It asks you to be active, to keep your paper trail, and sometimes to ask a Dutch friend or colleague to help with the letters. But there is a path, and for a small claim it costs little or nothing in legal fees. Knowing it exists already changes how you negotiate: write the agreement down before you start.
