The evening before Module 3 of Economic Cultures, I went through the project-budget deck one more time, and one thing stood out. Module 1 (My hourly rate) grounded the value of your work in who you are and how you live. Module 2 (From quote to invoice) priced your work for someone else, a client who buys it. Both look around you to define what money means in your life and your work. Module 3 (Project budget) turns that round.

A common thought about grant projects is that they're expensive, big and hard to get. That's not a misconception. The misconception is not realising that a substantial cultural grant is purpose-driven. It pays for a purpose, and for innovation grounded in that purpose. For artists, the form (or expression) of innovation is usually internal. Design projects are a bit more extroverted, with an eye on a problem and a new way to approach it.

In the Dutch creative industries, funds such as the Mondriaan Fonds and the Stimuleringsfonds respect a personal perspective on artistic and cultural innovation. A strong case for your purpose, made from a very personal position, is recognised and even celebrated. That makes this field quite unique. In South Korea, for instance, it works a bit differently. There, the same case might read as too personal and not practical enough; problem solving and the potential to scale up economically come first.

So the project-budget tool starts from the ambition, not from the money: first what you want to make and why, then what it really takes to realise, then who could pay for it. That is why these grants exist, and why you're supposed to approach them ambitious, daring and bold, not timid, shy and apologetic.

At the end of the exercise the number is often intimidating. Who is ever going to pay me this, so I can do this work? I saw the other side too. In the first two modules the graduates were very timid: they underestimated many things, and their budgets fell far short of their ambition. Neither is a mistake, and nobody is naive for it. Measured against your purpose, the number, however small or large, is a point to reflect on. If it's higher than you expected, take courage. If it's lower than it should be, be fairer to yourself.
